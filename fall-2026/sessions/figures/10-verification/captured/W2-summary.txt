# Savings, age structure and income growth (50 countries, 1960–70)

Across 50 countries in the 1960s, households saved more where incomes were growing faster: each extra percentage point of annual income growth went with a savings rate about 0.4 points higher (for example, 10.4% of income instead of 10%), after accounting for age structure and income level. Countries with more children saved less, with each extra percentage point of the population under 15 lowering the savings rate by about 0.46 points, while the share of elderly people and the level of income had no clear effect. Together these factors explain about a third of the differences in savings rates between countries, and the growth estimate rises to about 0.6 when Libya, whose 16.7% growth rate is far above every other country's, is left out.
