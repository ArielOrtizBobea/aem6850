# Life-cycle savings regression

In 50 countries over 1960-1970, households saved more where incomes were growing faster: each extra percentage point of annual income growth went with a savings rate about 0.4 points higher, comparing countries with similar age profiles and income levels (0.6 points if Libya, an extreme case of fast growth, is left out). Countries with more children saved less: 10 more percentage points of the population under 15 went with a savings rate about 4.6 points lower, as the life-cycle theory predicts, while the share over 75 and the level of income had no clear effect. These factors explain about a third (34%) of the differences in savings rates, which averaged 9.7% of disposable income.
