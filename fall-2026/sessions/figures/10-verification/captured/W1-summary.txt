# Savings, age structure and income growth

Across 50 countries in the 1960s, households saved more where incomes were growing faster: each extra percentage point of annual income growth goes with a savings rate about 0.41 points higher, holding the population's age mix and income level fixed. Countries with more children saved less, by about 0.46 points of savings rate for each extra percentage point of the population under 15, which fits the life-cycle idea that dependents draw down savings. The share over 75 and the level of income show no clear link to saving, and the four factors together account for about a third of the cross-country differences (R² = 0.34); the growth estimate rises to 0.61 when Libya, whose 16.7% growth rate is far outside the rest of the sample, is left out.
