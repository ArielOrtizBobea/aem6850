I've written `table6.csv` to the working directory. It has 24 rows, one for each line item on pages 8 and 9. The columns are Section, Item, Total, then the twelve Reserve Banks in printed order from Boston to San Francisco. Values are in millions of dollars with the thousands commas removed. The four Deposits sub-items are labelled "Deposits: …" so they can't be mistaken for top-level items. Footnote markers are dropped from the labels, and the footnote text itself (on page 10) isn't included.

**Rows where the twelve banks don't add to Total.** Seven rows are off, all by 1 or 2 (millions of dollars):

| Row | Total | Sum of 12 banks | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Coin | 1,514 | 1,513 | −1 |
| Central bank liquidity swaps | 157 | 159 | +2 |
| Interdistrict settlement account | 0 | −1 | −1 |
| Federal Reserve notes, net | 2,301,681 | 2,301,682 | +1 |

The other 17 rows add up exactly, including Total assets and Total liabilities and capital. Gaps this size are what you'd expect from rounding each bank's figure to the nearest million, and the note under the table says components may not sum to totals for that reason. I found no transcription errors.

**Interdistrict settlement account.** Each bank's figure on this row has a separate +/− sign printed just to its left. I stored them as signed numbers: Boston +23,560, New York −128,473, Richmond −75,487, Chicago −27,277, Kansas City −10,920, and the rest positive. Two checks support this reading. The row nets to −1 against a printed Total of 0. With these signs, each bank's asset items also add to its Total assets within rounding (Boston comes to 186,284 against a printed 186,283).

I also compared the parsed numbers against images of both pages, and they match.
