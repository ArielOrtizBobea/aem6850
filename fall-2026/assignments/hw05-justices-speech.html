HW5 · Do justices speak differently to the side they vote against?

Out Tue Nov 10 · due Tue Nov 17, 11:59 pm

AI assistance is recommended on this one. Use it, disclose it, and write the note yourself.

The question

At a Supreme Court oral argument, a lawyer for each side answers the nine justices’ questions for about half an hour. The petitioner asks the Court to overturn a lower court’s decision and the respondent defends it. Months later each justice votes for one side. Epstein, Landes & Posner (JOURNAL OF LEGAL STUDIES, 2010) found that the side the justices question more is more likely to lose. Dietrich, Enos & Sen (POLITICAL ANALYSIS, 2019) found that a justice’s voice is higher toward the side they go on to vote against. Measure both, and speed, for each justice in the 2023, 2024 and 2025 terms.

Target image

Data

  1. Arguments and recordings: the Oyez API, no key. https://api.oyez.org/cases?per_page=0&filter=term:2025 lists a term’s cases. Each case’s href gives its lawyers (advocates, with a description of the side each argued for) and its arguments (oral_argument_audio). Each argument’s href gives the MP3 (media_file) and the transcript: one section each time a lawyer takes the lectern, and in each section the turns, with speaker, start and stop in seconds, and the words in text_blocks. A speaker is a justice when their roles hold a role_title containing “Justice”; a lawyer named Roberts argues one case.
  2. Votes: the Supreme Court Database, a zipped CSV you download by hand. https://scdb.la.psu.edu/data/2026-release-01/, under “Justice Centered Data”, “Organized by Docket”. The site refuses scripts. One row per justice and docket: term, docket, justiceName, majority (2 with the majority, 1 in dissent), partyWinning (1 the petitioner won, 0 lost, 2 unclear) and vote (8 for an equally divided Court).
  3. Pitch: from the MP3s. Convert each to WAV with av and track the pitch with wrassp::ksvF0().

The three terms are 181 arguments on Nov 13 and 3.8 GB of MP3s. They were 183 on Nov 10, before Oyez moved two cases argued in October 2026 to the 2026 term. Save every file and never request one twice. Wait a second between requests.

How long it took me

About 15 minutes to download and 16 minutes to track the pitch, on a MacBook Pro with an M2 Max chip. No model is run.

Sides and votes

  • A section belongs to the lawyer who speaks the longest in it.
  • The lawyer’s description decides the side: petitioner, appellant or applicant on one side, respondent or appellee on the other. “Supporting …” overrides the party named: vacatur or reversal is the petitioner’s side, the judgment below is the respondent’s. Leave out lawyers supporting neither party.
  • About a dozen descriptions do not settle the side, mostly in cases that join several dockets. Rule those by hand in a small CSV, with your reason for each.
  • In a case that joins several dockets, use the docket Oyez files it under, for the vote and for who is the petitioner.
  • A justice voted for the petitioner when in the majority and the petitioner won, or in dissent and the petitioner lost. Drop recusals (majority empty), equally divided votes, cases with no clear winner and arguments SCDB has no decision for.

The measures

For each justice in each argument, toward the side they voted for and toward the side they voted against:

  • Length: the words they say to that side. A justice who speaks to one side only has zero words for the other.
  • Speed: words per minute, over their turns of 5 seconds or more.
  • Pitch: in Hz, the mean of the log pitch over the same turns where there is a voice, leaving out half a second at each end of a turn.

Speed and pitch count only when the justice has 30 seconds of such turns toward each side in that argument.

Output

  • figures/hw5_target.png, 11 × 5.8 inches at 200 dpi.
  • data_clean/justice_arguments.csv: one row per justice and argument, with the term, the docket, Oyez’s argument id, the justice, the vote, and the words, speed and pitch toward the side voted for and the side voted against.
  • NOTES.md: how many arguments you found, which ones have no vote and why, the sides you ruled by hand, and an AI disclosure (tools, uses, what you checked).

Figure rules

  • Three panels side by side, titled Length, Speed and Pitch, under one title.
  • One row per justice in order of seniority, the Chief Justice on top.
  • In each row, a check mark at the justice’s mean over arguments toward the side they voted for and a cross at their mean toward the side they voted against, joined by a line. One colour for the check marks and another for the crosses, the same in every panel.
  • Pitch is in percent: how much higher or lower the voice is toward the side voted against than toward the side voted for, compared inside each argument. Every check mark sits at zero.
  • A key centred under the panels. A dark background.

How to submit

Your repository is hw5-<your github username> in the course organization. Put the code in code/, with code/run_everything.R running it all, and paths relative to the project root. Push the code, data_clean/justice_arguments.csv, your CSV of hand rulings, the figure and NOTES.md. Keep the MP3s, the JSON files and SCDB’s file in data/ and list data/ in .gitignore. The grader does not download anything, so the script that draws the figure must run from a fresh clone with justice_arguments.csv alone. Commit as you go; the grader uses the last commit pushed before the deadline.
